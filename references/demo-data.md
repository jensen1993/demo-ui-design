# Scenario-specific demo data

The user needs to recognize their product and exercise its flow. Derive the sample data from the supplied brief, requirement document and existing project, not from an arbitrary industry chosen by the designer.

- Extract the actual actors, feature modules, fields, workflow states, terminology, currencies, units, required examples and business rules. If the document provides prices, tiers, discounts, thresholds, roles or status transitions, represent them faithfully where they are in the demo. Do not invent missing business rules and present them as facts.
- Build coherent fictional examples matching that context: appropriate names, items, categories, images, dates, values and states. Cover more than one realistic scenario or role where it helps demonstrate the requested functionality. When the industry or product category is genuinely unspecified, use clearly labeled neutral examples or ask only if that information is essential; do not assume a procurement system is agricultural because one invented sample is agricultural.
- Use provided user data and branding only within the requested scope. Do not present fabricated businesses, phone numbers, statistics, certifications, AI results or transactions as verified facts. Visually or contextually identify demonstration data, simulated integrations and uncertain assumptions.
- Keep relationships consistent across screens: a selected item should lead to its own detail and status; submitted forms and filters should update relevant lists; counts, prices, quantities, discounts, fees and totals should agree with each other and with stated rules. Preserve demo interaction state for the session; mention if a refresh resets it.
- Supply a useful data set of an appropriate size, not one generic card repeated everywhere. Where relevant include varied statuses, reasonable content lengths, representative filled and empty states, and searchable/filterable records. Sample data should support the requested workflows rather than inflate the scope.
- For requirement-document demos, map individual features and important fields, conditions, user roles and state transitions to screens and classify interactive simulation, display-only and omitted items; do not mark a whole module as interactive merely because its entry or a related screen exists. Avoid claiming feature completion when only an explanatory placeholder exists. Backend-dependent actions (payment, authentication, messaging, AI, etc.) are simulations unless actually integrated and authorized; never imply a real transaction occurred.

- Check sample interface copy and buttons against actual behavior: do not promise service details, payment, allocation, search, export, data persistence or other functionality that the demo does not actually show. Where an action is out of scope, label it as a simulated or future entry and say what it does. Check critical navigation, form validation, create/update actions and resulting list, details, totals and statuses.

## Product imagery and content quality gate

- For image-led scenarios such as marketplaces, food, services or property, use recognizably relevant, high-quality images by default for representative visible products and detail pages. Match the image to the named item, category and material/specification when visually meaningful: steel coil should look like coiled steel, a flange like a flange, and oil like packaged oil. Do not use generic line icons, colored blocks, unrelated stock images or repeat one generic image as the main product photo. Icons are appropriate for navigation, category shortcuts or a clearly labeled missing-image fallback. Non-image-led business interfaces do not need decorative photography.
- Source priority: assets supplied or authorized by the user; project assets with suitable rights; accessible assets with clear permission for the intended use; original/generated illustrations or images when available. Check licensing, attribution and redistribution requirements for the actual source, keep source/license records, and avoid hotlinking or logos without permission. If using generated imagery, mark it as illustrative when needed; do not portray it as an actual verified supplier, product photograph, certification or business claim. If suitable images cannot be obtained or generated, use the best clearly marked fallback, state the limitation and do not claim the imagery requirement passed. Never silently invent license provenance.
- Create a small representative content matrix from the brief before populating screens: product/service, matching image, specification, unit, sample price, quantity, region, supplier and workflow status as relevant. Use realistic variation and industry-appropriate terms, units and number formats. Reuse provided facts exactly, and label other values and entities as illustrative demo data rather than verified market prices, real companies or certifications. Do not fabricate real identities or contact details; use clearly fictional examples. Avoid claims of enterprise verification or actual stock unless explicitly simulated or supplied.
- Review representative list and detail screens against the matrix: item and image match, metadata fits the item, price/unit/quantity and totals are coherent, names/statuses stay consistent across navigation and search, image aspect ratio/cropping and readability work at target widths, and missing/failed images have an intentional fallback. Record image provenance, sample-data assumptions, examples inspected and any unresolved gaps in the delivery.
